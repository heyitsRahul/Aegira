# Aegira — one-page pitch

## Problem

Personal-safety apps make you choose between **help** and **privacy**. To get
help fast they track you continuously, store your location on their servers,
build social-graph profiles, and broadcast incident feeds that fuel vigilantism.
For the people who need safety tools most — women, gig workers, students, people
in coercive relationships — that standing surveillance is itself a danger, and
the apps don't work at all on the basic phones hundreds of millions actually
carry.

## Solution

Aegira makes every dangerous capability **session-shaped**: the power to see a
location or summon a responder exists only inside a consented, time-boxed
session, and is cryptographically destroyed when the session ends. No location
exists outside a session — anywhere. A duress PIN lies convincingly under
coercion. And it runs on a smartphone app, a no-JavaScript web page, **and over
plain SMS**, so any phone can call for help.

## Why now

- India's unified 112 emergency stack and cheap SMS/data make multi-channel
  coordination viable nationwide.
- Gig-economy and campus-safety buyers now treat worker/student safety as a
  procured benefit with budget.
- Post-GDPR/DPDP, "we never hold your location" is a feature buyers and
  regulators reward, not a cost.

## Who it's for (beachhead → expansion)

- **Beachhead:** one segment with acute need and a payer — e.g. a university
  campus, or a gig platform's field workforce (delivery/ride/home-services).
- **Expansion:** employers (safety as a benefit), women's-safety NGOs, insurers,
  and eventually direct-to-consumer in low-trust-in-police markets.

## Business model

B2B2C. Individuals won't reliably pay for rare-event safety; **organizations
will pay to keep their people safe.** Per-seat/yr for campuses and employers;
per-active-worker for platforms; the consumer tier stays free to maximize the
safety network. (Direct-to-consumer is the distribution flywheel, not the
revenue.)
